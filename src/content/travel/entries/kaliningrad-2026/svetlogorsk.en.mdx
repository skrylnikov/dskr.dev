Today I went for a walk around Svetlogorsk.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-1" />

Rauschen was founded in 1258 and became a resort in 1820. After the war, the town was renamed Svetlogorsk.

The town surrendered without a fight during the war, so many old houses have survived along its narrow, winding streets.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-2" />

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-3" />

The tower and its mud baths were built in 1908. The baths used seawater and apparently still operate. Only military personnel can use them, though.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-4" />

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-5" />

The train from Kaliningrad takes about an hour. Almost all the platforms here are low, and it turns out Lastochka trains have retractable steps. These trains came from Moscow's Central Diameters network: they haven't even removed the signs :) You can also come across a Moscow tram in Kaliningrad.

The station building dates from 1975.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-6" />

Right behind the station is a cable car built in 1984 that takes you down to the promenade. Before that, there was a funicular here dating from 1908.

Part of Svetlogorsk stands on a sand dune. Trees were planted to stabilize it as the town was built.

The dune stands right in front of the Baltic Sea and makes getting down to the water rather difficult.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-7" />

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-8" />

The promenade is being renovated. There is also a sundial built in 1974. For some reason, it wasn't working today :(

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-9" />

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-10" />

In 2014, an elevator was built to take people from the promenade up the dune. You have to pay for it, but it is much easier than taking the stairs :)

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-11" />

The German writer E. T. A. Hoffmann lived in Rauschen.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-12" />

The lake is home to the SvetloYozhiki: pictured here are Pykhtik, Malinka, Grandpa Hedgehog, and Bul the fish.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-13" />

There are ducks and swans too, along with vending machines selling food for them.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-14" />

Svetlogorsk is Russia's only Slow City.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-15" />

Someone in Soviet times didn't like the front door opening straight onto the street corner, so it was bricked up and a statue of a fisherman put in its place.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-svetlogorsk-16" />

The private Wheel of History museum. This seems to be a common problem with private museums: lots of fascinating and varied exhibits, and not a single label.
