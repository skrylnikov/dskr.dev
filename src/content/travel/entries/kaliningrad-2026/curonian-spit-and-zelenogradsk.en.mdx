Today I visited the Curonian Spit and Zelenogradsk.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-1" />

An interesting place overall, but the actual Dancing Forest is literally just a few dozen trees. You can only walk along the paths, and the paths are fenced in. Kills the vibe.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-2" />

The spit is sandy and consists of a whole series of dunes. Trees were planted here to stabilize them too.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-3" />

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-4" />

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-5" />

Zelenogradsk is another seaside resort, founded in 1252. It was lucky during the war too, so plenty of old houses have survived.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-6" />

There are also loads of cats in town, and everyone loves them. The town administration even has a chief cat officer.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-7" />

Cats have monuments here. They are painted on buildings and manhole covers, and appear on signs.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-8" />

The cats have their own crossing and little houses. The houses stand on a heated pavement.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-9" />

The town is beautiful. Unfortunately, the tour left us very little time, so I only got to see one street.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-curonian-spit-and-zelenogradsk-10" />

I rounded off the evening with a tasting of beer, stroganina, cheese, meat, and eel.
