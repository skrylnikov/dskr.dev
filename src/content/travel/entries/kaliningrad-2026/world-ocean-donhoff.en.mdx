The Museum of the World Ocean in Kaliningrad is a whole complex of buildings and ships. A new building, Planet Ocean, opened here in 2025. That was the one I wanted to visit, but figuring out the tickets turned out to be tricky.

When I was buying my ticket, sales opened on Mondays and tickets were already sold out by Tuesday. I managed to book the “Ocean: The Cradle of Life” tour on level 2. Levels 0 and 1 were open for self-guided visits, but my ticket didn't include levels 4, 6 or 7 — those required different tours. The [building itself is opening in stages](https://www.world-ocean.ru/planeta-okean), so it's worth checking which spaces your chosen tour covers before buying a ticket.

My tour started at 2:30 p.m. Until then, I decided to explore as many ships as I could. Luckily, there was a combined ticket for them, and there were hardly any visitors on board.

## The Irbensky lightship

Irbensky was built in Finland in 1962. It's one of the last surviving lightships. In 2017, it was brought to the museum's waterfront in Kaliningrad.

## Vityaz

The ship that would become Vityaz was built in Germany in 1939. After the war, it passed to Britain, then to the USSR in 1946, where it was converted for scientific research. In 1957, an expedition aboard Vityaz recorded a depth of 11,022 meters in the Mariana Trench.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-world-ocean-donhoff-11" />

The ship is huge, and I only had half an hour before my tour. After about twenty minutes of wandering through the cabins, I had just one goal left: finding the exit. Luckily, I made it in time.

## Planet Ocean

The tour covered the evolution of life in the ocean, from single-celled organisms to whales.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-world-ocean-donhoff-14" />

The aquariums are home to all sorts of fish. Each tank has a label explaining which sea, river or ocean it represents.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-world-ocean-donhoff-15" />

In the large hall, a huge screen was showing a video about the sea. There was a light organ there too. And yes, some of the tours here include concerts.

<MediaGallery gallery="kaliningrad-2026-world-ocean-donhoff-16" />

Overall, I liked the museum, though the crowds and the trouble with buying tickets took some of the shine off the visit. I'm glad we have museums on this scale in Russia. I'd also have liked to explore the ships with a guide.

My daytime tour lasted less than an hour, and some rooms were off limits. On the other hand, it's easier to explore the outdoor areas during the day. And taking photos is easier in daylight too.

The fort has been a museum since 2015. There's plenty of commercial activity on the grounds too: a shooting range, a coffee shop, a café and a merch shop. I arrived late, though, so almost everything was already closed.
